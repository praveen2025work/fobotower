Agent One Finance · FOBO break investigation

AOF does the checking and the write-up on every FOBO break. The controller makes every decision.

Think of a junior analyst who prepares each break the same way every morning, never skips a check and writes down every figure, but never signs anything off.

AOF
  • Fetches the breaks and the data the controller would look up
  • Runs the team's checks on every break
  • Sorts breaks by cause and proposes what to do
The AI
  • Looks only at breaks the checks could not explain
  • Writes up what it found, with the evidence
  • Suggests, never decides
The controller
  • Checks the sorting before the AI starts
  • Approves or rejects every proposal
  • Nothing is posted or sent without them

What a break is

Front office and back office should agree on P&L. When they don't, that is a break.

FO P&L (CATS)=BO P&L (MOTIF)+FOBO adjustments

Every evening MB Rec compares the two for each book and lists the breaks. The controller's job is to find out why each one exists and what to do about it. The rule from the team's investigation skill: restore the equation truthfully, never force it with an adjustment.

What changes

Same checks, same decisions. AOF does the legwork.

Today, for each break, by hand
  1. Open CATS, MOTIF and MB Rec and pull the figures
  2. Run the checks in the FOBO skill one by one
  3. Decide: post an adjustment, don't post and send it to the team that must fix it, or watch it
  4. Write up why, and sign off
With AOF, for the whole book at once
  1. AOF pulls the figures from the same systems
  2. AOF runs the same checks on every break, and records each result
  3. AOF groups the breaks by cause and proposes a decision for each group, with the reason
  4. The controller reviews the proposals and signs off

For controllers

Your morning with AOF

  1. Your book is waiting, already worked. When MB Rec finishes its end-of-day run, AOF opens the day's case for each book, fetches the data and runs the checks.
  2. Check the sorting. Before the AI is asked anything, you see each break, what the checks found, and the groups. Add what the desk told you, then continue. Or stop the run with a reason.
  3. Review the proposals. One per cause: what was found, the evidence, the proposed decision and who owns the fix. Approve the clear ones together. Anything marked "needs your judgement" gets a proper look.
  4. Sign off. You answer the same completion questions as today, and can reject with a reason. AOF records it all and sends a ticket to the team that must fix each cause.

For management and risk

Why it is safe

  • A person decides every group. AOF proposes. Nothing is posted and no ticket is raised until a controller approves.
  • The AI never decides. It sees only what the checks could not explain, may use only the data sources it is given, and must show its evidence.
  • The rules are the team's own. They come from the FOBO investigation skill. Changing them needs two owners: one proposes, another approves.
  • Some rules can't be overridden. A cause in front office is never posted as an adjustment. That is enforced by the system, not left to the AI.
  • No guessed thresholds. A check that needs a limit nobody has confirmed shows as "not run", never as passed.
  • Everything is on record. Each check, figure, reason and decision is kept, with who made it and when.

Say it in a minute

Two short scripts

For management

Every morning MB Rec gives our FOBO controllers a list of breaks: places where front office and back office disagree on P&L. Today a controller works each break by hand: pulls the figures from several systems, runs the same checks, decides and writes it up. AOF does the checking and the write-up for the whole book, using the team's own investigation skill, the same way every day. It groups the breaks by cause and proposes what to do. Where the rules can't explain a break, the AI investigates and shows its evidence. Nothing is posted or sent without a controller approving it, and every step is recorded. In our test run, five breaks with £514,506 at stake became four prepared proposals; the controller approved three and sent one back with a reason. The next use case, such as cash reconciliation or accruals, is a new set of rules on the same platform, not a new system.

For controllers

When MB Rec finishes, your book is already in your inbox, worked. First you check the breaks and how AOF sorted them, and add anything the desk told you. Then AOF finishes the investigation. You get one proposal per cause: what it found, the evidence, what it suggests and who owns the fix. Approve the clear ones together; look properly at anything marked "needs your judgement". You answer the same sign-off questions as today, and you can reject with a reason. You still make every decision. AOF does the legwork and keeps the record.
